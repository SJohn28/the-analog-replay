# Journey — *Escape*: A Track-by-Track Review

Some albums are remembered for a couple of hit singles. Journey’s *Escape* gives you plenty of reasons to keep listening after the familiar songs have finished. It brings together heartfelt ballads, energetic rockers, and performances that show how well these five musicians worked together.

Steve Perry’s voice is a huge part of the appeal, but everyone contributes something memorable. Neal Schon’s guitar adds excitement and feeling, Jonathan Cain’s piano helps shape the album’s softer moments, and Ross Valory and Steve Smith keep the rock songs moving with confidence.

What makes *Escape* such an enjoyable listen is the variety. It can be romantic, reflective, or ready to turn up the volume, sometimes within the space of a few songs. The hits deserve their reputation, but the rest of the record deserves attention, too.

## 1. “Don’t Stop Believin’”

There’s a reason people still turn this song up when it comes on. From the first piano notes, “Don’t Stop Believin’” creates a feeling of possibility. It speaks to anyone who has wanted something more from life, taken a chance, or needed a little encouragement to keep going.

Steve Perry brings warmth and conviction to the story. He makes its people and places easy to picture, giving the song a human connection that reaches beyond its famous refrain. As the band gets louder and Schon’s guitar takes flight, it becomes almost impossible to resist singing along.

Even after countless radio plays, sporting events, and late-night singalongs, the recording still has life in it. It opens *Escape* with optimism and excitement, setting a high bar for everything that follows.

## 2. “Stone in Love”

“Stone in Love” gets things rocking with a guitar opening that grabs your attention immediately. It has the excitement of looking back on a youthful romance—the kind of memory that can feel close enough to touch, even years later.

Perry captures that mixture of affection and longing without letting the song lose its energy. There is joy in the performance, along with the knowledge that those days are gone.

Schon’s guitar is a highlight, especially toward the end, when he gets more room to stretch out. Valory’s bass playing also gives the song plenty of life. Put it all together and you have a track that makes you want to turn up the stereo and stay with it until the last note.

## 3. “Who’s Crying Now”

“Who’s Crying Now” slows things down and looks at the hurt and frustration that can build between two people. The piano sets a thoughtful mood, and Perry sounds like someone trying to make sense of a relationship that has become painful.

The song is easy to get caught up in because it doesn’t overdo the emotion. There’s sadness in the singing, but also pride and a sense that neither person wants to be the one who gives in.

Schon’s guitar solo near the end is one of the track’s best moments. It is easy to hum along with and carries the feeling of the song long after the singing stops. This is Journey showing how powerful a quieter performance can be.

## 4. “Keep On Runnin’”

“Keep On Runnin’” brings a welcome shot of energy after the more serious mood of “Who’s Crying Now.” It’s fast, lively, and full of the confidence that makes Journey’s rock songs so much fun.

Smith’s drumming helps drive it along, while the guitars give Perry something to dig into. His singing has more bite here, matching the band’s enthusiasm.

This is the kind of song that makes sense at concert volume. You can picture the crowd on its feet and the band enjoying every minute. It also keeps the album moving, arriving just when it needs a burst of noise and excitement.

## 5. “Still They Ride”

“Still They Ride” is one of the album’s more thoughtful songs. It brings to mind familiar streets, old friends, and the memories people carry with them as their lives change.

Perry gives the words plenty of feeling, allowing the song to unfold at its own pace. He doesn’t need to sing at full power all the way through to hold your attention. Some of the best moments come when he pulls back and lets a phrase linger.

There’s a bittersweet quality here that becomes more affecting with repeated listens. It may take a little longer to become a favorite than some of the bigger hits, but it has a way of staying with you.

## 6. “Escape”

The title track brings back the feeling of wanting to break free and see what lies beyond the life you know. Schon’s guitar gives it an urgent start, and the band keeps that sense of restlessness alive throughout.

There’s a lot happening in this song, but it never feels difficult to follow. Just as you get comfortable, the band changes things up and gives you another reason to pay attention. Perry ties it all together with a performance full of determination.

“Escape” is a good reminder of how much this band could do beyond its radio hits. It’s adventurous, exciting, and one of the tracks that makes listening to the entire album worthwhile.

## 7. “Lay It Down”

“Lay It Down” is one of the tougher-sounding songs on the record. The guitar grabs you first, and the rest of the band comes in with enough force to keep you listening.

Perry meets that energy with some powerful singing, while the chorus gives the song the catchy quality you expect from Journey. It manages to rock hard and still be easy to sing along with.

There’s no long wait for things to get going here. The band gets straight to the point and keeps the excitement up. For anyone who knows Journey mainly through its love songs, “Lay It Down” offers a good reason to explore further.

## 8. “Dead or Alive”

“Dead or Alive” is a quick, spirited rocker that sounds like the band having a great time. It races along with lively piano, plenty of guitar, and an enthusiastic performance from Perry.

Cain’s playing adds a little old-fashioned rock ’n’ roll flavor, giving the song a personality of its own. Everyone seems caught up in the same burst of energy, and that enjoyment comes through.

It’s over quickly, but that suits it. The song delivers a few minutes of fun before the album takes a more serious turn. It may not be the first title people mention when talking about *Escape*, but it earns its place.

## 9. “Mother, Father”

“Mother, Father” is one of the most emotional songs on *Escape*. It deals with a family in trouble, and Perry sings with a sense of concern and desperation that makes the subject feel personal.

His voice is extraordinary here, especially when he reaches for the higher notes. What makes the performance memorable, though, is how much feeling he puts behind them. You hear someone trying to reach people who are hurting.

The rest of the band helps make those moments land. The quieter stretches draw you into the story, and the louder passages give the emotion somewhere to go. It’s a dramatic song, and Journey gives it everything.

## 10. “Open Arms”

“Open Arms” closes the album with one of Journey’s most heartfelt performances. It begins simply, with Cain on piano and Perry singing, giving you a chance to settle into the words and melody.

As the rest of the band joins in, the song grows without losing that personal feeling. Perry sounds vulnerable and hopeful, capturing the uncertainty of opening yourself up to someone you love.

The song’s appeal comes from how directly it expresses those feelings. It doesn’t take much effort to connect it to a person or a moment in your own life. After all the energy and emotion that come before it, “Open Arms” gives *Escape* a warm, satisfying finish.

## Final Verdict

*Escape* has the songs most listeners remember, but it also has the depth to make a full listen rewarding. The rockers bring excitement, the ballads give you something to connect with, and the band sounds comfortable moving between the two.

Perry delivers some remarkable singing, and the musicians around him give every song its own character. There’s always another guitar passage, piano part, or vocal moment worth coming back to.

Whether you’ve known it for decades or are hearing it all the way through for the first time, *Escape* offers much more than a trip through a few famous hits. It’s an album that earns its place on the turntable from beginning to end.
